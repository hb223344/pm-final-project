# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** Lena, 32, a heavy pay-later user who shops mostly on her phone, has two or three pay-later apps installed, and lives on a middling salary with little cushion.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** She wants to always know exactly what she owes and when it’s due, all in one place, so payday never brings surprises.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** In the test, her card balance and her normal pay-later orders showed up on two different screens, so she couldn’t tell which total was the real one. On top of that, her card statement date and pay-later due dates didn’t match, so she got two reminders in the same week for different amounts. Instead of making things simpler, the card became one more thing to check before payday. (N-02, N-07, T-103, T-104)
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Klarna and PayPal apps, the Riverty app (two separate screens), her bank app, and reminder emails or texts. The bank app is a guess, the others come from the data.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** The process

Before payday, she opens each pay-later app to see what’s due.
In the Riverty app, she checks the card balance, then the pay-later orders, and adds them up in her head.
She compares two reminders from the same week to work out what’s due when.
When the numbers don’t match, she asks support which total is the real one.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment she sees two different totals in the Riverty app and two reminders in the same week for different amounts, and can’t tell what she actually owes.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** “I could not see in one place what I owe on the card and what I owe on my normal pay later orders.” (N-07)
Backed by ticket T-103: participants ask which total is the real one.
